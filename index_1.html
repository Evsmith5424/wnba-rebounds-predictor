Methodology & validation

The model is a linear regression on eight predictors: the player's rebounding average over her last 15 games, her rebounds per minute played, her shrunk rebounding average specifically against the selected opponent, the opponent's team defensive rating, the opponent's rebounds given up per game, the opponent's own field goal percentage (misses create rebound chances, so a colder-shooting opponent means more boards up for grabs), estimated game pace (the average of both teams' possessions per game), and projected minutes for this game.

Why rebounds instead of points as the template: rebounding turns out to be a substantially more predictable stat than scoring — the model's R² here is meaningfully higher than the points version's, which lines up with how rebounding depends more on role, positioning, and playing time (all fairly stable game to game) and less on shot-making variance, hot streaks, or how a defense chooses to guard someone.

Projected minutes defaults to the player's recent average and is trained on realized minutes played from her own game log. Drag it down for a rest day, foul trouble, or an injury designation, or up for heavy expected minutes; rebounds scale with playing time about as directly as any box-score stat does.

Matchup shrinkage: the head-to-head rebounding average is shrunk toward the player's last-15 average by a factor of n / (n + K), where n is the number of prior meetings against that opponent and K = (chosen by cross-validation). With zero meetings it's just the last-15 average.

Likelihood of X rebounds: exactly the same empirical approach as the points tool — every 2026 prediction was made with 5-fold cross-validation, producing real (predicted, actual) pairs split into four buckets by predicted-value quartile, and the odds for your line are the share of comparable past predictions that landed at or above it.

Read the numbers honestly: a chronological holdout test gives a mean absolute error of about rebounds, versus rebounds for just guessing "her recent rebounding average." Treat the predicted range, not the single point estimate, as the real output.

Coverage: players have a fully reconstructed 2026 rebounding game log (real last-15 average, real rebounds/min, and real head-to-head history vs every opponent they've faced). The remaining players in the dropdown use their real season-to-date rebounding average as a stand-in for the last-15 figure and rebounds/min, and fall back to that season average (rather than true head-to-head data) for the matchup feature — flagged as estimated below the prediction.
